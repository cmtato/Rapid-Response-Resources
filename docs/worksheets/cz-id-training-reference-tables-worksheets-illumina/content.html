CZ ID Training: Reference Tables

QC Metrics Table

QC Metric

Definition

Passed Filters

The percentage of reads remaining after host filtering, quality filtering and subsampling, compared to the initial reads input. Tells you how many reads were used for final analysis.

Passed QC

The percentage of reads that passed the quality filtering thresholds imposed by fastp. Gives you an idea of the sequencing quality.

Total Reads*

The total number of reads uploaded.

DCR*

Duplicate Compression Ratio; The ratio of sequences present prior to running CZID dedup (duplicate removal) vs after duplicate removal.

ERCC reads*

The total number of reads aligning to ERCC (External RNA Controls Consortium) sequences. The number of ERCC reads will be proportionally higher in samples with relatively low input RNA as compared to those with high amounts of input RNA.

SubSampled Fraction*

After host filtration and QC, the remaining reads are subsampled to 1 million fragments (2 million paired reads). This field indicates the ratio of subsampled reads to total reads passing host filtration and QC steps.

* Fields are not default in the samples table and need to be added by clicking on the column selector

Sample Metrics Table

Report Metric

Definition

Score

Score is only calculated if a background model is applied. It is CZ ID's heuristic for ranking microbial hits. The score is intended to combine the following aspects of the evidence for a hit: (a) species-level information, (b) genus-level information, (c) information about relative abundance within the sample, (d) information about abundance relative to the chosen background controls. The score is calculated as follows: ((abs(genus NT Z) * species NT Z * species NT rPM) + (abs(genus NR Z) * species NR Z * species NR rPM))

Z

Z-score statistic is only computed if a background model is applied. It is used for evaluating the prevalence of microbes in your sample as compared to background controls. The Z-score is computed based on the specified background model.

rPM

Number of reads aligning to the taxon in the NCBI NT/NR database, per million reads sequenced

r

Number of reads aligning to the taxon in the NCBI NT/NR database

contig

Number of assembled contigs aligning to the taxon in the NCBI NT/NR database

contig r

Total number of reads aligning to all assembled contigs for this taxon

%id

Average percent-identity of alignments to NCBI NT/NR

L

Average length of the local alignment for all contigs and reads assigned to this taxon

E value

Average Expect value (e-value) of alignments to NCBI NT/NR. The Expect value (e-value) is a parameter that describes the number of hits one can "expect" to see by chance when searching a database of a particular size. The closer to 0 the better.

Passed QC

>50%

You can be relatively confident in the quality of the sequencing data.



<10%

Samples with low % Passed QC likely contained many short sequences that were associated with adapter dimers or sequences that obtained low-quality scores during base-calling. If the Passed Filters value is below 20%, you should work to understand why reads were filtered out (see next section) and use this information to improve library preparation steps. Interpret results from these samples with caution.



*Outliers

Take note of any values above that stand apart from the others because of very high or very low Passed QC values. These should be noted for their unusually high or low sequencing quality and looked into more closely.

  • Samples with a known microbiome (ie Stool, NP Swab, Environmental Samples, Sputum) tend to have a lower DCR due to the complexity of the samples and abundance of total nucleic acid.

Sample Name(s)

Next Steps/Notes

Passed Filters

>10%

A higher Passed Filters value is generally positive as it indicates that you have a greater amount of reads to analyze for non-host taxa.


What is the Sample Type for each of the samples?

  • Samples with a known microbiome (ie Stool, NP Swab, Environmental Samples, Sputum) tend to have a higher number of non-host reads due to greater microbial content in the samples.

  • Sterile site fluids (Serum, CSF, Plasma) tend to have fewer non-host reads and greater numbers of host or ERCC reads.

  • Whole Blood tends to have a distribution of non-host reads in between these two extremes due to the high abundance of host cells.

<5%

It is not uncommon to see Passed Filters values below 1% for samples with high abundance of host reads.


*Outliers

Take note of any values that stand apart from the others because of very high or very low Passed Filters values. These should be noted for their unusually high or low amount of non-host data and looked into more closely.
